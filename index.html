Vernier Caliper: Theory & Metrology Principles

1. Working Principle & Jaws Functions

A standard Vernier Caliper features three distinct precision metrology interfaces:

  • Lower External Jaws: Used to measure outer dimensions (diameters, lengths, widths).
  • Upper Internal Jaws: Placed inside hollow bores, pipes, and tubes to measure internal diameter (ID).
  • Depth Rod (Blade): Extends from the main beam tail to measure the depth of holes, blind wells, and beakers.

2. Least Count (LC) Formula

Least Count = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 1 MSD / n
  • 0.1 mm precision: 10 VSD = 9 MSD (9 mm) → 1 VSD = 0.9 mm → LC = 1.0 − 0.9 = 0.1 mm.
  • 0.05 mm precision: 20 VSD = 19 MSD (19 mm) → 1 VSD = 0.95 mm → LC = 1.0 − 0.95 = 0.05 mm.
  • 0.02 mm precision: 50 VSD = 49 MSD (49 mm) → 1 VSD = 0.98 mm → LC = 1.0 − 0.98 = 0.02 mm.

3. Taking a Reading

Observed Reading = Main Scale Reading (MSR) + [Coinciding Vernier Division (VSR) × LC]

4. Volumetric Calculations

  • Hollow Pipe Wall Thickness: $t = \frac{OD - ID}{2}$
  • Hollow Pipe Material Volume: $V_{mat} = \frac{\pi (OD^2 - ID^2) L}{4}$
  • Solid Cylinder Volume: $V = \frac{\pi D^2 L}{4}$
  • Sphere Volume: $V = \frac{\pi D^3}{6}$
