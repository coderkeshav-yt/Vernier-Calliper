Vernier Caliper: Theory & Metrology Principles

1. Working Principle

A Vernier Caliper operates by aligning $n$ Vernier Scale Divisions (VSD) with $(n - 1)$ Main Scale Divisions (MSD). Because each VSD is slightly shorter than 1 MSD, the difference between them defines the Least Count of the instrument.

2. Least Count (LC) Formula

Least Count = 1 MSD − 1 VSD = 1 MSD / n
  • 0.1 mm precision: 10 VSD = 9 MSD (9 mm) → 1 VSD = 0.9 mm → LC = 1.0 − 0.9 = 0.1 mm.
  • 0.05 mm precision: 20 VSD = 19 MSD (19 mm) → 1 VSD = 0.95 mm → LC = 1.0 − 0.95 = 0.05 mm.
  • 0.02 mm precision: 50 VSD = 49 MSD (49 mm) → 1 VSD = 0.98 mm → LC = 1.0 − 0.98 = 0.02 mm.

3. Taking a Reading

Observed Reading = Main Scale Reading (MSR) + [Coinciding Vernier Division (VSR) × LC]

4. Zero Error Correction

When the jaws are closed completely flush:

  • Nil Error: Vernier 0 mark aligns perfectly with Main Scale 0 mark.
  • Positive Zero Error: Vernier 0 lies to the right of Main Scale 0. Correction: Subtract error from observed reading.
  • Negative Zero Error: Vernier 0 lies to the left of Main Scale 0. Correction: Add error to observed reading.
Corrected Reading = Observed Reading − (Zero Error)
